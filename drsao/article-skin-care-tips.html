A dermatologist's daily skin routine

You do not need a 10-step routine — just the right basics, done consistently.

Skin Care  ·  5 min read  ·  Reviewed by Dr. Prajct Sao

Good skin is less about expensive products and more about a simple routine followed every day. For Indian skin and weather, three steps form the foundation of healthy skin: cleanse, moisturise and protect. Here is how to build a routine that actually works.

Step 1: Cleanse

Washing your face removes dirt, sweat, oil and pollution that build up through the day. Use a gentle cleanser suited to your skin type — twice a day is usually enough. Over-washing or harsh scrubbing can strip the skin and trigger irritation or extra oil.

Step 2: Moisturise

Every skin type needs moisturiser, including oily skin. A moisturiser keeps the skin barrier healthy and comfortable. Choose a light, gel-based formula for oily skin and a richer cream for dry skin.

Myth check: Skipping moisturiser does not "dry out" oily skin in a good way — it can actually make the skin produce more oil to compensate.

Step 3: Protect with sunscreen

Sunscreen is the single most important anti-ageing and anti-pigmentation step. Use a broad-spectrum sunscreen of at least SPF 30 every morning, and reapply during the day if you are outdoors. This applies even on cloudy days and indoors near windows.

A simple daily routine

Morning

  • Gentle cleanser
  • Moisturiser
  • Broad-spectrum sunscreen

Night

  • Gentle cleanser
  • Any doctor-prescribed treatment, if applicable
  • Moisturiser

Habits that help your skin

  • Drink enough water and eat a balanced diet
  • Get adequate sleep — skin repairs itself at night
  • Avoid picking or squeezing pimples, which can cause scarring
  • Introduce new products one at a time so you can spot reactions
  • Be patient — most skincare results take several weeks

When to see a dermatologist

A basic routine works well for general skin health, but some concerns need professional care — persistent acne, pigmentation, rashes, sudden changes in the skin, or any condition that affects your confidence. A dermatologist can tailor a treatment plan to your skin.

Important: This article offers general guidance only. Active ingredients and prescription treatments should be used under a dermatologist's advice.
