7 common causes of hair fall

Understanding why hair fall happens is the first step to controlling it.

Trichology  ·  5 min read  ·  Reviewed by Dr. Prajct Sao

Losing around 50 to 100 hairs a day is completely normal. But when you notice more hair on your pillow, in the shower or on your comb, it is worth understanding what may be behind it. Hair fall rarely has a single cause — here are seven of the most common.

1. Genetics (pattern hair loss)

The most common cause of long-term hair thinning is hereditary pattern hair loss, which affects both men and women. It tends to progress gradually and runs in families. Early treatment usually gives the best results.

2. Stress

Significant physical or emotional stress — an illness, surgery, major life event — can push many hairs into a resting and shedding phase. This type of shedding is often temporary and improves once the stress is managed.

3. Nutritional deficiencies

Hair needs the right fuel. Low levels of iron, vitamin D, vitamin B12, protein or other nutrients can weaken hair and increase fall. A balanced diet and, where needed, corrected deficiencies can make a real difference.

Tip: Crash diets and very restrictive eating are a frequently missed cause of sudden hair fall. Sustainable, balanced nutrition supports healthier hair.

4. Hormonal changes

Thyroid imbalance, PCOS, pregnancy and changes after childbirth can all affect the hair cycle. Addressing the underlying hormonal issue often helps the hair recover.

5. Scalp conditions

Dandruff, fungal infections and inflammatory scalp conditions can damage the environment in which hair grows. Treating the scalp is an important part of treating hair fall.

6. Hairstyling and harsh treatment

Tight hairstyles, excessive heat styling, strong chemical treatments and rough handling of wet hair can all contribute to breakage and hair loss over time.

7. Certain medical conditions and medicines

Some illnesses and medications can cause hair shedding as a side effect. If your hair fall started around the same time as a new medicine or illness, mention this to your doctor.

When should you see a dermatologist?

Consider a consultation if you notice:

  • Sudden or patchy hair loss
  • A widening parting or visibly thinning scalp
  • Hair fall along with itching, redness or scaling
  • Hair fall that continues for more than a few months
Important: This article is for general awareness only. A dermatologist can identify the exact cause through examination and tests, and recommend the right treatment for you.
